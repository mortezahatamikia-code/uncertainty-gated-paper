\section{Conclusion and Future Directions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{BioDINO-Edge}, an on-device few-shot framework for foliar plant disease diagnosis that operates without gradient computation or backpropagation. By coupling frozen vision foundation models (DINOv2 Small) with botanical vision--language encoders (BioCLIP ViT-B/16), the architecture forms a joint 896-dimensional descriptor capturing localized lesion morphology alongside taxonomic context. Regularized Fisher-LDA with Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage resolves the small-sample covariance singularity ($N \ll d$), completing parameter adaptation in 533.99\,ms on an ARM64 CPU. At inference, a dual-metric Shannon-margin uncertainty gate routes routine foliage through a 59.78\,ms Tier-1 path while reserving deeper multimodal consensus and transductive recalibration for ambiguous queries, reducing overall latency to 149.84\,ms per leaf. Across 30 benchmark episodes, BioDINO-Edge achieved 97.41\% $\pm$ 0.71\% accuracy on PlantVillage with a 0.702\% false-negative rate, and reached 84.22\% $\pm$ 11.24\% in 5-way 5-shot field evaluation on PlantDoc (50.79\% across all 27 classes). Its transductive recalibration trails semi-supervised LaplacianShot by 1.08\,pp on the controlled split when the full test pool is available, but that margin is confined to the laboratory benchmark: labelling one leaf at a time costs the propagator 3.00\,pp, and on PlantDoc BioDINO-Edge leads by 18.65\,pp in 27-way and 22.29\,pp in 5-way evaluation.

\subsection{Future Directions}
Limitations and their scope are set out in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. Work in progress follows two technical directions. First, we plan to compile the frozen visual backbones to dedicated edge Neural Processing Units (NPUs) using mixed-precision INT4/INT8 quantization via Qualcomm QNN and MediaTek NeuroPilot delegates. Because NPU architectures process dense matrix operations at lower power, this can reduce Tier-2 latency below 30\,ms and support continuous video-rate diagnosis. Second, we aim to integrate BioDINO-Edge into the closed-loop controller of an autonomous robotic sprayer, linking real-time foliar classification to selective variable-rate nozzle actuation in commercial crop fields.
