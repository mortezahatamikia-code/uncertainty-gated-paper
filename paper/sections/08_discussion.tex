\section{Engineering Applications, Practical Deployment, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Practical Deployment on Agricultural IoT and Mobile Hardware}
BioDINO-Edge is designed for deployment on power-constrained edge platforms, including handheld scouting devices, smart tractor booms, and agricultural UAVs. Eliminating backpropagation avoids GPU memory spikes and on-device training heat, executing feed-forward inference and closed-form algebra directly on the CPU. Given thermal throttling under continuous multi-core execution (settling to 4.26\,FPS under sustained load; Section~\ref{sec:edge_hardware}), the architecture is designed specifically for shutter-triggered, per-frame scouting inspections rather than continuous video streams. Two-tier packaging allows deploying the $\approx$22.8\,MB base application over cellular connections, downloading the companion module only when multimodal escalation is required.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Research Directions}
BioDINO-Edge processes images at the individual leaf frame level. In unsegmented field imagery with dense, overlapping canopies, multiple leaves from neighboring plants can enter the camera field, lowering gating confidence. While tight lesion cropping degrades host context, processing unsegmented canopy clutter introduces irrelevant background vectors. Future work could incorporate hierarchical foliar parsing to segment individual leaves prior to closed-form classification. A further statistical limitation is that the multi-view support expansion produces correlated pseudo-replicates: the analytical Ledoit--Wolf intensity assumes i.i.d.\ sampling, so the nominal $N-C=429$ degrees of freedom overstate the independent information content of the support set (effective sample size between 55 and 440). The sensitivity study in Section~\ref{sec:aug_sensitivity} confirms that pure replication deflates the estimated intensity by $\approx$8$\times$ without adding information, yet downstream accuracy remains stable across view counts and augmentation types; a cluster-aware shrinkage estimator that explicitly discounts within-image dependence remains future work. Finally, frozen vision transformers can exhibit attention artifacts on non-symptomatic structures like veins or petioles \cite{darcet2024vision}. Because Fisher-LDA operates on pooled representations downstream, it does not alter internal attention; the framework targets whole-leaf diagnosis rather than pixel-level lesion localization.

A further practical limitation concerns degraded inputs. Severe defocus blur attenuates fine lesion textures, reducing diagnostic accuracy. The Android client screens frames before diagnosis with two inexpensive guards: the maximum cosine similarity between the DINOv2 embedding and the class prototypes must exceed 0.12 for the frame to count as foliage, and the mean luminance of a 64$\times$64 center crop must exceed 22/255 for it to count as illuminated; either failure zeroes the reported confidence so that no diagnosis is announced. A discrete-Laplacian variance check additionally flags motion blur below a variance threshold of 45. Because that check only warns, defocused frames can still be processed, and the uncertainty gate acts as a router rather than an abstention mechanism: ambiguous queries escalate to Tier-2, and abstention is available only in the optional selective mode (Table~\ref{tab:tier_routing}). Extending the guard to blur-aware abstention with refocus prompts remains future work. We did not assemble a labelled non-leaf corpus, so the rejection thresholds are engineering settings rather than measured operating points.

\textbf{Scope and threats to validity.} The edge measurements come from a single physical device (Samsung Galaxy S24~FE) with CPU-only INT8 ONNX Runtime; absolute latency and memory will differ on other SoCs, although the algorithmic cost and the delegate-agnostic pipeline do not. The controlled comparison (Table~\ref{tab:comparative}) isolates the adaptation algorithm on identical frozen features, and Table~\ref{tab:fair_baselines} additionally re-runs three transductive few-shot methods and three 896-D inductive comparators on those same features. One of them, LaplacianShot, is 0.53\,pp more accurate than our transductive mode in the fixed-pool protocol, so we do not claim superior transductive accuracy there. What the batch-size study in Section~\ref{sec:comparative_results} shows is a different operating envelope: propagation requires the whole query set before it can label anything and collapses to 76.90\% when only 100 queries are visible, whereas recalibration degrades only from 97.59\% to 95.27\% over the same range because it labels each leaf from the support set and its anchors are drawn within the visible batch. The PlantDoc protocol uses labeled target-domain support; a true source$\rightarrow$target cross-domain transfer (e.g., PlantVillage$\rightarrow$PlantDoc), quantitative image-quality stress tests, and multi-device profiling remain future work.
